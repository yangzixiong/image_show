About ImageLab

A browser page that reads pixel and depth values out of your images — why it exists, how it works, who keeps it running.

Why it exists

Two problems keep coming up when you slice sprites, match colours or read depth data: most online tools want you to upload the image to someone else's server, and the rest simply do not open 16-bit TIFF. The first is unrealistic for unreleased assets; the second locks out exactly the files whose numbers you need. ImageLab aims to be just a page: open it, drop an image, read values — the file stays on your device the whole time.

How it is built

No backend and no build step. The page is a handful of local files: rendering is plain Canvas 2D, and TIFF decoding is handled by the bundled UTIF.js:

<script src="/lib/utif.js"></script>
<script src="/js/decoder.js?v=3"></script>

The full path of one lookup:

  1. choose, drop or paste an image — the browser reads the file locally;
  2. everyday formats go through native decoding, TIFF goes through the bundled UTIF.js;
  3. the decoded pixels land on a canvas where zoom, pan and the pixel grid all happen;
  4. hover to read coordinates, colours or depth, click to copy — no network request at any point.

Who made it and how to reach them

ImageLab is designed and developed by yangzixiongh. For bug reports, suggestions or collaboration, email yangzixiongh@163.com or leave a message at github.com/yangzixiong; the details are on the FAQ page.

What we promise

No image collection, no sign-up, open and use. Images are never uploaded or written to disk, and the only thing stored locally is a language preference. The technical basis and our log handling are spelled out line by line on the privacy page.

What comes next

The three guides and help pages (depth maps, colour picking, pixel inspection, FAQ) are done. Next up: broader image format support and more documentation. To try it right away, open ImageLab.
