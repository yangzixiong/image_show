Social Media Image Sizes and Crops

One photo, four platforms, four different frames. Decide the ratio and the pixel count first, then choose what to cut. Doing it the other way round means starting over.

Common sizes, by platform

These are the sizes people usually publish at, not hard platform rules. The upload dialog is the final authority.

1080 px on the long edge is enough for most feeds. Larger files get recompressed anyway, and smaller files cannot regain detail when enlarged. A 20 MP camera original does not need to be published at 20 MP; the platform only keeps what its own pipeline allows.

Ratio and pixel count are separate decisions

4:5 is a shape. 1080 × 1350 is one grid that fits that shape. So is 2160 × 2700 — same shape, four times the file size. Written out:

4:5  =  1080 × 1350  =  2160 × 2700
9:16 =  1080 × 1920  =  1440 × 2560

Pick the ratio first, then the long edge. For social posts, 1080 to 1440 covers most cases. Keep a larger long edge only when the image will be printed or edited again. The crop tool offers original, 512, 768 and 1024 as long-edge options. "Original" exports exactly the pixels inside your frame; 1024 scales that frame so its long edge becomes 1024.

Three ways to ruin a crop

Stretching. Drag a 4:3 photo into a 1:1 box and faces get wider, circles turn into ovals. The platform will not fix it; it displays the pixels you uploaded. Cut the sides off instead of squashing the image.

Re-saving JPEG after every step. Each save runs another lossy pass. Crop, add text, save again, and edges start to smear. Keep intermediate files as PNG and convert to JPEG once at the end.

Cropping a screenshot. A screenshot has already been scaled by the browser and compressed by the screen capture. Pixel boundaries are soft. Use the original file when you can, especially for product shots or print.

Locking a ratio in the crop tool

  1. Drop the image into the crop tool, or pick a file. Decoding happens in the browser; nothing is uploaded.
  2. Choose 1:1, 4:3, 3:4, 16:9, 9:16, 3:2 or 2:3. The selection keeps that ratio while you drag its edges.
  3. Move the frame. Keep the face and the important information inside first, then adjust composition. Anything outside the frame is discarded. You can redraw the frame, but there is no undo history.
  4. Set the long edge. 1024 is enough for most feeds; choose "Original" when you need the source detail.
  5. Pick PNG or JPEG. PNG is required for transparency; JPEG is smaller for photographs. Check the output dimensions shown by the tool before exporting.

Freehand selection is fine for a draft and bad for delivery. A hand-drawn box is often 1.03:1, and the platform then applies its own crop on top. For anything you publish, pick a fixed ratio.

Composition: protect the face, then the message

For a portrait crop, leave a little space above the head and place the eyes near the upper third. For a landscape, avoid putting the horizon through the exact centre.

On a 9:16 cover, captions, account names and buttons cover the lower part of the frame, and the interaction column takes the right side. Keep text and key objects in the middle-upper safe area. A 1:1 profile picture gets a circular or rounded mask, so keep the important mark near the centre.

Product shots are the exception: do not crop tight. Leave 8–10% of background around the object so a platform's white border or rounded corners do not clip it. That margin also makes the thumbnail easier to read.

Choosing an export format

PNG is lossless and suits text, line art and transparency. JPEG is smaller and suits photographs. WebP is smaller still, but older systems may not open it. The crop tool exports all three and defaults to PNG.

Do not export, re-import and export again. Every conversion shifts colour and edge detail slightly. If you need versions for several platforms, crop each one from the same original rather than cropping a 1:1 export into 9:16.

FAQ

How are 1080 × 1350 and 4:5 related?

4:5 is the shape; 1080 × 1350 is one pixel grid with that shape. Divide 1080 by 4 to get 270, then multiply by 5 to get 1350. Any dimensions with that ratio work.

My original is only 800 px wide. Can I export 1080?

Yes, but the extra pixels are interpolated. They add no real detail. It is fine for a feed and obvious in print or a second crop. When the source is small, choose a smaller long edge.

Why is it cropped again after I upload?

Feeds, preview cards and detail views can use different ratios. A 4:5 upload may still preview as 1:1 in a timeline. Keeping the subject centred with margin is more reliable than matching one exact pixel size.

Does cropping change colours?

Cropping itself does not change pixel values. Colour shifts come from export format, colour profile or the platform's recompression. For colour-critical work, export PNG and check the source colour space.
