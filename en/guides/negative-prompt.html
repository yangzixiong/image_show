How to Write Effective Negative Prompts

Everyone writes negative prompts, yet piling on words almost always backfires. This guide explains what they actually do and how the fewest words exclude the most certain problems.

Reverse guidance, not a filter

The sampler never "removes" what the negative prompt names — it pushes each step away from that description. A negative prompt is a set of directional instructions: precise words aim the push at the problem, vague words spread it thin across the whole semantic space, which amounts to no push at all.

One constraint is often overlooked: positive and negative share the CFG budget. When the positive prompt grows, the negative quietly weakens — the usual reason a once-reliable negative "stopped working" yesterday. Check whether the positive got longer first.

Three categories, three jobs

CategoryTypical wordsWhen to include
Qualitylowres, bad quality, jpeg artifactsKeep resident — useful almost everywhere
Structureextra fingers, bad anatomy, mutationAdd when people or hands are in frame
Scene exclusionwatermark, text, logoPer-image only — not worth keeping resident

The three categories follow different rules: quality words can stay resident; structure words track the content; scene words belong only where the problem actually appeared — forcing watermark into a model that never emits one just dilutes everything before it.

How many words

Five to twenty words is the working range. Every word claims a little attention, and in a 100-word negative only the first few still bite — a short negative is the stronger one. Weights work here too: (deformed:1.3) means "exclude harder", ideal when exactly one word needs more force.

Keep a resident negative and per-image additions apart: fold quality words into the resident list (or use an embedding such as EasyNegative, one token standing in for a whole phrase) and append only two or three scene words per generation. Short, and far easier to attribute when something goes wrong.

An embedding is the other compression route: hundreds of negative concepts trained into a single token (EasyNegative and BadDream are exactly this), placed at the head of the negative to stand in for a whole phrase, with scene words following it. The trade-off is black-box behaviour — a mismatch with your model or style injects artefacts instead of removing them, so A/B it in isolation: one image off, one image on. If results get consistently worse, drop it and return to plain words.

Symptom table: check here first

SymptomCheck firstAdjustment
Oversaturated, painterly lookNegative too strong or duplicated in positiveRemove same-name words, lower individual weights
Broken hands or facesStructure words missingAdd structure terms; if unchanged, raise resolution or switch sampler
Stray text in backgroundScene exclusions missingAppend watermark, text
Flat, grey contrastNegative harming the positive subjectCompare word by word against the positive for conflicts

One more misread is common: the fault sits in the positive prompt. If the positive already says masterpiece and best quality, bad quality in the negative fights it head-on — the two cancel while burning budget. Keep quality words on one side only; the positive is the more stable home.

A full pass with the tools

  1. Open the negative word bank and pick candidates by scenario (portrait / anime / photoreal / general quality);
  2. Use prompt split to peel the negative half out of a long prompt and maintain it on its own;
  3. Or borrow a full structure with a built-in negative from the random generator, then trim it against the symptom table above.

For positive-side writing and how weights interact, see Prompt Weighting (a:b): Syntax and Tuning — the weight syntax in negatives is identical. Once split, read the list end to end: the same word twice (blurry appearing two times) does not double the effect — delete duplicates and the list shrinks again.

FAQ

Are more negative words always better?

No. Past twenty words they dilute each other and only the first few act. Maintain a resident quality layer plus per-image scene words, kept within two lines.

Can negative prompts use weights?

Yes, with the same syntax as the positive prompt. (blurry:1.4) in the negative means exclude blur harder.

Why doesn't adding bad hands help?

Diminishing returns — structure failures mostly come from resolution, sampler or the model itself. After several broken frames, change settings before adding words.

Are txt2img and inpaint negatives the same?

Not quite. Inpainting has already fixed everything outside the mask, so drop the full structure list and keep only words conflicting with the masked target.
