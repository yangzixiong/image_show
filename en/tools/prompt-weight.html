    Bracket rules and explicit weights

    (word) boosts by ×1.1 by default, [word] divides by 1.1, {word} nudges up by ×1.05, and (word:1.3) sets an exact multiplier — negatives and decimals are allowed. Nesting multiplies layer by layer, so ((word)) is about ×1.21. Switch to the ComfyUI dialect and (word) no longer carries ×1.1; [a|b] and {a|b|c} are flagged as alternation — in ComfyUI they cycle through variants instead of weighting one term.

    Five kinds of problems, one click to locate

    An unclosed bracket, mismatched pair types, a stray closing bracket, a weight that is not a number (such as (word:abc)), or nesting deeper than 8 layers — all appear under Problems on the right. Click any line and the textarea selects exactly those characters; the list clears the moment you fix them. A stray closing bracket never stops the parse — it stays in the text and still gets counted.

    Reading the band, estimating tokens

    Grey-blue marks strong breaks at ≤0.5, blue anything below 1.0, white exactly 1.0, orange the common 1.1–1.5 boost zone, and red anything above 1.5, where artefacts often start to appear. The token figure is a CLIP-rule estimate: it splits on BREAK, counts two special tokens per segment, and is enough to tell whether a segment approaches the 77-token ceiling.
