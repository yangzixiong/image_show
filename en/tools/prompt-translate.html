Prompt Translator — 中英对照

Turn Chinese tags into the English tags models actually recognise, or work in reverse. Input is split on commas, enumeration marks and newlines and translated term by term; fixed spellings such as 1girl and 8k stay whole instead of being translated word by word. Anything missing from the table is passed through untouched and listed separately so you can decide whether to add it.

Translation (comma tag list)

How the table matches

The term table ships with the page offline. Each entry carries a Chinese headword, the English tag, synonyms and a fixed-spelling flag. Lookup tries the headword first, then synonyms — 单人 and 独自 both land on solo, 虚化 and 背景虚化 both land on bokeh. English matching ignores case, so mixed input that is half Chinese and half tags still sorts itself out.

Why fixed spellings must stay whole

1girl, 2girls, 8k and 35mm are atomic tags the community agreed on: translating 1girl into “one girl” adds two tokens the model never learned, and 8k becomes “eight k” just as useless. The fixed-spelling flag locks these entries to whole-word matching on both sides, so they never enter a word-splitting path and always travel through the translator unchanged.

What happens to uncovered words

Terms without an entry are passed through verbatim — a missed translation is safe, a wrong tag can wreck the whole image. They also appear under “Not covered” below: if one turns out to be common, add it to data/prompt-terms.json and the next update will match it; one-off names and coined terms are fine left as they are, since most frontends simply ignore tags they do not know.
