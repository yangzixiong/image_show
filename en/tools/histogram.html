How to read it

The horizontal axis is luminance from 0 to 255 and the vertical axis counts how many pixels fall on each value. A curve bunched on the left means a dark frame, bunched on the right means a bright one, and a peak in the middle means normal exposure. In RGB overlay mode whichever channel sits on top dominates that range; grey areas are where all three overlap.

Clipping percentages are the honest part

When the ends of the histogram are cut flat, those pixels are already pure black or pure white and the detail is gone. This tool counts 0–4 as shadow clipping and 251–255 as highlight clipping and reports both as percentages. Above roughly 1% you should treat that area as unrecoverable and either go back to the raw file or reshoot with a different exposure.

Depth map mode

Open a 16-bit TIFF depth map and the histogram becomes single-channel: the horizontal axis is the depth range in millimetres, the vertical axis is how many pixels sit at each depth, and zero-valued pixels are counted separately as invalid rather than folded into the distribution. It is the quickest way to see whether a depth map is concentrated in one plane or has large holes.
